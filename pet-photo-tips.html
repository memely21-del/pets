How to take a great photo of your pet for a portrait

A portrait follows the photo it comes from: the sharper your pet's eyes and the softer the light, the more the painting looks like your pet. You only need a phone.

1. Use soft daylight

Stand near a window or go outside on a cloudy day or in the shade. Turn the flash off: it makes eyes glow and flattens the fur.

2. Get down to their eye level

Kneel or sit so the phone is at the height of your pet's eyes. Photos taken from above make the head look big and the body small.

3. Focus on the eyes

Tap your pet's eyes on the screen to focus there. If the eyes are sharp, the portrait will feel alive.

4. One pet, no people

Take the photo of one pet at a time, with the face clearly visible and no people in the picture. It is also the kind of photo our portraits need.

5. Fill the frame, skip the filters

Get close enough that your pet fills most of the photo, and send it without filters or stickers, so the colors of the fur stay true.

6. Take a few and pick the sharpest

Pets move. Take several photos in a row (a squeaky toy or a treat above the phone helps them look at you) and keep the sharpest one.
